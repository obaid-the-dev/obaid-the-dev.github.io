What AI Dev Planner Actually Builds: Web Development Services, and What Makes Them Different

Custom web apps, SaaS, MVPs, AI systems, mobile apps, portals, and vibe-coded app rescue — all founder-led, with written scope, your repository from day one, and a 60–90 day warranty.

By Obaid Tariq, Founder & Lead Engineer, AI Dev Planner · 18 November 2026 · Originally published on Substack · Also on LinkedIn

Most web development agencies sell you a website. AI Dev Planner sells you working software — a website, a web app, a SaaS platform, or an AI system — built to be used every day, owned by you, and able to grow without a rebuild.

Here is what that actually means, service by service.

1. Custom Web Development

Not a template with your logo on it. A custom website or web application built around how your customers actually use it — your workflow, your content, your calls to action.

Who it is for: a startup or growing business that has outgrown a template, or whose product cannot fit one.

What you get: a fast, mobile-first site or web app on a modern stack, including Next.js and React, designed to grow into a web app, a SaaS platform, or a customer portal later — not a site you have to rebuild when version two arrives.

2. SaaS Development

Multi-tenant SaaS platforms, from first release to scale: subscription billing, team management, role-based access, and the architecture that lets one codebase serve many customers safely.

Who it is for: founders building a subscription product, or a business turning an internal tool into a product others pay for.

What you get: multi-tenancy, billing, and access control from day one, not bolted on later. A typical SaaS MVP is $25,000–$65,000 over 8–12 weeks, scoped in writing before building starts.

3. MVP Development

A fixed-scope, production-grade first release — not a demo, not a six-month project that never ships.

Who it is for: a founder who needs to validate an idea with real users, or a team that needs to prove a product before committing a full budget.

What you get: a written scope that says what version one includes, what it deliberately leaves out, and what "done" looks like. Then a launchable product in weeks, not months, with a plan for what comes after launch.

4. AI Engineering

Custom AI applications, enterprise AI integration, and autonomous agents — built with guardrails first.

That means: connecting enterprise language models, vector search, and private retrieval (RAG) pipelines into your existing software and databases without rewrites; and deploying agents that can execute business workflows with tool-calling, execution guardrails, and human-in-the-loop approvals.

Who it is for: a business with real data and a real workflow to automate — not a company looking for an AI demo.

What you get: secure AI systems with clear limits, checked outputs, and a human where it counts. If an AI feature cannot be trusted with your customers' data, AI Dev Planner will say so — or say "not yet."

5. Mobile Applications

Cross-platform iOS and Android apps, built with the same backend as your web product, so your customers get the same data and the same experience on every device.

Who it is for: a product that needs to work on the go, offline, or in the field — not just on a desktop.

6. Customer Portals and Internal Tools

Secure client hubs, custom CRMs, administrative panels, and workflow automation — the systems that replace spreadsheets, retyping, and chasing updates by message.

Examples from the services page: a patient portal that cuts front-desk scheduling calls, a custom CRM without per-seat fees, and client hubs with document vaults, billing history, and role-based permissions connected to your CRM or ERP.

Who it is for: clinics, recruiters, logistics teams, and any business where the team is the integration between three tools.

What you get: one place where the truth lives, with audit logs and access controls — not another dashboard to check.

7. Vibe-Coded App Rescue

Your prototype — built with Lovable, Bolt, Cursor, or another AI tool — works in the demo and breaks in real use. AI Dev Planner audits it, repairs authentication, fixes leaked secrets and database security, and rebuilds the unstable parts into production code.

Who it is for: a founder with a stalled or fragile AI-built app who does not want to start over.

What you get: a review first, then fixes in the right order. Most of it is usually fine. A few parts are not. Starting over is the expensive answer. Reviewing first is the honest one.

8. UI/UX Design

Design that starts with the job the user is trying to finish — not with a mood board. Responsive layouts, interactive Figma prototypes, and accessibility to WCAG AA standards, so the product is usable, not just pretty.

How these services are different from the others

Most agencies differ in what they say. AI Dev Planner differs in how the work is done. This is the delivery model listed on aidevplanner.com/services:

Senior-led, not handed off. Your software is designed and built by senior architects with 10+ years of production experience. No junior-only delivery, no third-party contractors, no explaining your vision five times to five account managers. You talk directly to the engineers building your product, in a dedicated Slack or Teams channel.

Written scope and exclusions, before you pay. Every milestone includes an explicit written deliverable list and clear exclusions before work begins. That is what prevents scope creep and keeps budgets predictable.

Milestone-based delivery. Payments are tied to agreed working software milestones. You review and test functioning deliverables on staging before you sign a milestone off.

100% repository and cloud ownership, from day one. All code is committed directly to your GitHub or GitLab repository from day one. Infrastructure runs in your AWS, GCP, or Vercel accounts, with zero vendor lock-in. Your code and IP are assigned to you in a signed written assignment.

Bi-weekly working-software walkthroughs. Every two weeks you get a live demonstration and a recorded video walkthrough of working software on staging — not a status report.

Comprehensive documentation. Schema diagrams, API contracts, deployment instructions, and architecture decision records, so your team can maintain the product after handover.

Security by default. OWASP Top 10 standards, strict database Row Level Security, encrypted secrets management, and role-based access control — not security added as an extra.

60–90 day post-launch warranty. Every project includes a dedicated 60-to-90-day post-launch warranty covering bug fixes and operational support. Launch is when real users find real problems. That period is planned, not improvised.

The delivery pipeline behind it is the same every time: discovery and requirements, technical feasibility and architecture, scope, milestones and cost, UX and system design, development in two-week sprints, QA, security and testing, deployment and handover into your own cloud, then ongoing improvements and support.

Who this is for — and who it is not for

This is for a startup founder, or a growing business, that needs a real product launched, owned, and supported — and is willing to scope it honestly first.

It is not for a buyer who needs a 500-person delivery team, large-scale regulatory compliance, or an in-house team on site every day. A larger firm may fit better there, and AI Dev Planner says so plainly.

It is also not for a buyer who wants the cheapest possible website. A template is cheaper. It is also the reason many founders pay twice: once for the cheap build, and again to fix what it got wrong.

A simple test for any agency

Before you sign with anyone, including AI Dev Planner, ask:

  • Who exactly will build my product, and will I talk to them directly?
  • What is included, what is not, and what does it cost in total?
  • Do I own 100% of the code and IP, in a signed written assignment, with the code in my own repository from day one?
  • What happens in the first 90 days after launch?
  • What will my customers be able to do after using this that they cannot do now?

If an agency cannot answer those clearly, walk away — no matter how big its portfolio is.
